# Licensing FAQ

**Is QuoxCORE open source?** No. QuoxCORE is proprietary source-available software under the [Quox Community Source Licence](../../LICENSE). Its source may be inspected, but core modification, repackaging and redistribution are restricted.

**Is it free?** Yes. Unmodified QuoxCORE may be run on your own infrastructure in production, including inside a for-profit business, without per-seat or server fees.

**Can I build and sell plugins?** Yes. Plugins, agents, skills, integrations, workflows and other independent extensions built through documented interfaces belong to their creators and may have commercial or open-source licences. Quox LTD does not automatically own them.

**Can I charge for services using Quox?** Yes. Selling your own outputs, reports and services is different from selling access to Quox as a hosted platform.

**Can I run a Quox service for client organisations?** Not as your managed/multi-customer Quox platform without a commercial agreement. Installing or supporting a customer's own official Quox instance as their contractor is separately allowed within the licence boundaries.

**Can I modify or remove the Quox logo?** Not on protected QuoxCORE product surfaces. Your own organisation names, tenant/plugin views and output branding may be configured through supported mechanisms. White-labeling is commercially licensable.

**Can I fork the public GitHub source?** GitHub's terms may permit forks within GitHub. That is not a licence to patch, redistribute or sell protected QuoxCORE.

**Will the proprietary licence become Apache automatically?** No. Quox Community Source Licence 1.0 has no fixed conversion date. Earlier BSL versions are governed by their own historical licence grants.

**Are the protocols and SDKs restricted?** No. They are separately licensed Apache 2.0 / MIT projects. Read their own licences.

**Does installing free Quox phone home?** The licence does not require an account or mandatory telemetry for the free self-hosted platform. Optional paid services can have separate terms.
